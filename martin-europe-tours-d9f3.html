Your hotel's beach club

Grand Hotel Tritone, Praiano · included with your stay · guests only

Worth saying plainly before you book anything: you already have one. The Tritone's beach club sits directly below the hotel, cut into the cliff, and it's reserved for guests. You get down by lift through the rock, or by the staircase the hotel's founder had carved into the cliff face — locally known as the impossible staircase, and worth doing once for the walk itself.

Down at the water there are loungers, a bar, and Ninò, the beach restaurant, open daily for lunch by the sea. There's a dock, so boats can collect you from the water rather than making you climb back to the road — useful on days you've booked something that starts from the sea.

Treat this as the default and pay for a beach day elsewhere only when you want the crowd and the noise, which is a real preference and not a wrong one. That's the honest difference between the two here.

See the hotel's beach

One Fire Beach, Cala della Gavitella

Praiano · about a 15-minute walk · open May to October, 10am–8pm

The beach club in Praiano, on the cove at Gavitella, and the one that gets the afternoon sun when the rest of the coast has gone into shadow. It's cliffside rather than sandy — you go off the rocks straight into deep water, with a lifeguard on duty, showers, and changing rooms behind you. The kitchen runs on what's in season, and there's a full bar.

Sunbeds are sold by row, and the row is the decision. Sea View is the front, Exclusive is second, Orange puts you in the third or fourth where the noise is. The PPC package is the top tier, with champagne service and a set tasting menu at lunch. At 4:30 every afternoon they run Melon Time, which is exactly as chaotic as it sounds — worth knowing whether you want to be there for it or gone before it.

You reach it on foot from Praiano or by water; there's a dock for private boats, and guest shuttles run to and from Positano. The walk down is stairs rather than a stroll, so plan the return accordingly.

Cantine Marisa Cuomo, Furore

About 15 minutes away · tours at 9:00 and 10:00 · booking required

The closest serious wine to where you're staying, and the one I'd send you to if you only do one. The estate sits above the Fiordo di Furore, with vines on terraces so steep they're worked by hand, and a cellar dug into the rock face. Fiorduva, the white they're known for, comes off those terraces.

Two ways to do it. The guided tour of the production area, the rock cellar, and a vineyard is free, runs at 9:00 or 10:00, and has to be booked ahead — it's shared with whoever else has booked that slot. Or book a tasting at the restaurant beside the cellars, which makes the visit private and adds the wines paired with local food over a lunch or dinner sitting. Tasting packages start at €60 per person.

Those two morning slots are the whole schedule, so this needs to be planned into a day rather than dropped in. It's roughly fifteen minutes from you — I can arrange a driver, or a taxi from the hotel does it.

Paros
Four tour types · six experiences
▾

Paros is the island Greeks go to when they want the Cyclades without the queue. Naoussa is the pretty fishing harbor turned evening scene; Parikia is the port town with the oldest church in Greece sitting quietly behind the ferry noise; the interior is marble villages and olive terraces. Rent a car for at least two days — the island rewards driving.

Boat days aren't listed here yet on purpose. Paros runs dozens of them — catamarans, traditional wooden boats, private charters, half days and full days, out to Antiparos, Despotiko, and the coves you can't reach by road — and the right one depends on your group and how much of a day you want to give it. Paros by private car with a local driver

Pickup at your hotel, the port, or the airport · sedan, van, or minibus · itinerary is yours
From $161.81 Per person

Paros rewards driving more than any of your three stops — the interior is marble villages and olive terraces that the bus network barely touches. This puts a local driver and a vehicle sized to your group on it for the day, so you cover the island without anyone navigating a hire car on lanes built for donkeys.

The usual run takes in Naoussa, the old fishing harbour that fills up in the evening, and Lefkes, the inland capital built out of sight of the water because pirates couldn't see it from the sea. Between them are the viewpoints and the beaches — Kolymbithres and its wind-carved granite, Santa Maria, Golden Beach on the windward side. Stop where you want, for as long as you want; the driver works around you.

The sensible way to use it is as your one land day, with the boat day booked separately. It also solves the airport or port run at either end if you time it right — worth asking about when you book.

Inland villages, clay beach, and a fishing harbour

Lefkes · Prodromos · Kalogeros · Piso Livadi
From $200.79 Per person

The inland half of Paros, which most visitors never see because the ferries and the beach clubs are all on the coast. You start in Lefkes, the old capital up in the mountains, built out of sight of the water because pirates couldn't spot it from the sea, and walk a stretch of the Byzantine Trail — a marble-paved path that has connected these villages for centuries. Then Prodromos, smaller again and still working.

From there down to Kalogeros, the beach people go to for the clay in the cliff face rather than the sand — you can smear it on, dry off in the sun, and swim it back off. The tour finishes at Piso Livadi, a fishing harbour on the east coast where the boats still come in, with the option of coffee or a long lunch by the water before you head back.

This covers the same ground as the private island tour but with a guide and a fixed route rather than a driver waiting on you. Take one or the other, not both.

Antiparos: the cave, Agios Georgios, and the old town

Half day · transport and English-speaking escort included
From $212.60 Per person

Antiparos sits ten minutes across the channel and runs at a slower speed than Paros. The cave is the reason most people go: a descent of around 400 steps into a chamber of stalagmites where Lord Byron carved his name, and the temperature drops the moment you're inside.

From there to Agios Georgios on the south coast, looking across at uninhabited Despotiko, where the Archaic sanctuary is being reassembled column by column — a quiet spot for a swim or a long lunch by the water. The day finishes in Antiparos town, one marble street of tavernas and shops with the old castle at its centre.

Transport and a driver-escort are included throughout, which is what makes it a half day rather than a logistics exercise with two ferry crossings in it.

You're standing on the rim of a volcano that blew apart around 1600 BC. Around six hours · private · tastings with food · sunset upgrade available

Santorini's vines are trained into low woven baskets called kouloura, coiled on the ground to shield the fruit from the wind and catch moisture from the sea fog. Some root systems are centuries old. This is private and runs about six hours, with two or three estates depending on the option you pick. Each has its own setup and pour, and the tastings come with food rather than crackers. A driver matters here: the estates are spread across the island's interior and the pours add up.
