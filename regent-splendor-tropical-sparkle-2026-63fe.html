The week before Christmas, and the week after

Drive to the port. No flights, no transfers, no airport mornings.

Every rate below is for one suite, two guests, on the same sailing: Seven Seas Splendor out of Galveston on Friday, December 11, home on Monday, December 28. Nine ports, seven sea days, Christmas Day in Grand Cayman. The ship is the constant; the only decision is which suite you'd like to live in for seventeen nights.

I've laid out five categories in the order they're priced. Two of them are the same suite with different perks, and I've tried to say plainly what each step up actually buys.

Seven Seas Splendor

All suites, all balconies, 746 guests, and a fare that already includes most of what other lines charge for.

Splendor entered service in 2020 and is the sister ship to Seven Seas Explorer: 748 guests, 548 crew, every one of the 374 suites with a private balcony and a marble-and-stone bathroom. The fare covers specialty dining (Compass Rose, Prime 7, Chartreuse, Pacific Rim, Sette Mari), wine and spirits throughout the ship, gratuities, unlimited WiFi, valet laundry, 24-hour in-suite dining, and unlimited shore excursions in every port. None of that changes between the categories below.

What does change is the suite itself (size, bed, bath), and whether your category sits at the Concierge level, which adds a pre-cruise hotel night, priority reservations, and a handful of in-suite extras. Explore Seven Seas Splendor →

Five categories, three physical suites, two levels of service. Pick the one you'd like to come home to each evening.

Option 1 · Category G1 · Deluxe Veranda Suite · 1 suite · 2 guests

Deluxe Veranda Suite

The entry point: compact, well-finished, and the only category with a shower rather than a tub.
Deluxe Veranda Suite · photo courtesy Regent Seven Seas Cruises

At 252 square feet inside plus an 88-square-foot balcony, this is the smallest suite on the quote and still larger than many lines' balcony cabins. The bedroom is separated from the sitting area by a curtain rather than a wall; the sitting area holds a sofa, a coffee table sized for in-suite dining, and a desk. Floor-to-ceiling glass opens onto the balcony.

The honest trade: a European queen bed rather than a king, a glass-enclosed shower instead of a bathtub, a built-in closet rather than a walk-in, and WiFi for two devices. For seventeen nights, the question is how much time you expect to spend in the suite versus on the ship and ashore. This category is currently sold as GTY only: Regent assigns the specific suite closer to sailing, on Deck 6 or 7.

Option 2 · Category F2 · Serenity Suite · 1 suite · 2 guests

Serenity Suite

The same footprint as Concierge, without the Concierge extras. Forward on Decks 7–9.
Serenity Suite · photo courtesy Regent Seven Seas Cruises

Here the suite grows to 331 square feet inside, with a balcony of 83 to 132 square feet depending on the exact location. The bed is a European king and faces the floor-to-ceiling windows, so the first thing you see in the morning is the sea. The bathroom has dual sinks and a tub; the closet is a walk-in.

Against the Deluxe Veranda, the extra $1,000 per person buys roughly 80 more square feet, the king bed, the tub, and the walk-in closet. Against the Concierge Suite above it, you give up the pre-cruise hotel night, priority reservations, and the in-suite extras, and keep roughly $500 per person. Like G1, this category is currently GTY: Regent assigns the suite, on Deck 7, 8, or 9.

Option 4 · Category E · Concierge Suite · 1 suite · 2 guests

Concierge Suite

The Serenity footprint plus the Concierge level: a hotel night before you sail, and first call on reservations.
Concierge Suite · photo courtesy Regent Seven Seas Cruises

Physically this is the same 331-square-foot suite as the Serenity, with the king bed facing the balcony, dual sinks, and a walk-in closet, in a palette of creams and blush. What changes is the service tier. Concierge suites include a one-night pre-cruise hotel package with transfers, breakfast, and porterage; priority access to online shore excursion and dining reservations; WiFi for four devices; an illy espresso maker, cashmere blankets, binoculars, and a Regent gift; and 10% off ultra-premium wine and liquor plus 5% off Regent Choice excursions and land programs.

On a seventeen-night sailing, priority reservations are the practical perk: Prime 7 and Chartreuse fill quickly, and Concierge guests book before the general opening. Category E sits on Decks 6 through 9, forward, midship, and aft, and you can choose a specific suite rather than taking a GTY assignment. Regent is showing two suites left at this fare.
