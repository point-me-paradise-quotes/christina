The trip

One family, two ways to spend a week at sea

This page lays out two cruises leaving from Galveston in June 2027 so everyone in the family can read about both, compare them honestly, and tell us which one they prefer. Both sail from the same pier, both come home to Galveston, and both are built for a group spread across several staterooms. Sailing A

MSC Seascape — Western Caribbean, 7 nights

June 6–13, 2027 · Galveston round trip

Seascape is a big, bright ship with an indoor promenade that runs the length of the lower decks and a long open-air walkway outside. The rhythm of this week is easy to read: two sea days to settle in, three ports back to back, then one more sea day to pack and sleep late before a 6:30 a.m. arrival in Galveston.

The ports are the familiar western Caribbean trio, and each one earns its spot for a family. Costa Maya is a purpose-built pier with a pool and shops at the gate; the real draw is twenty minutes south in Mahahual, where beach clubs put loungers in the sand for the price of lunch, or an hour inland at the Chacchoben ruins. Roatán is the island with the reef closest to shore — at West Bay you can snorkel out from the beach without a boat. Cozumel is the most developed of the three, with taxis lined up at the pier for Chankanaab, Palancar's beach clubs, or the main square in San Miguel for tacos and shopping.

Staterooms on Seascape

MSC prices its cabins in three tiers. Bella is the entry rate — you choose the cabin type and MSC chooses where on the ship it goes. Fantastica lets you pick a deck range. Aurea is the top tier, with balcony staterooms on decks 9–15. Every stateroom is priced two ways — fare only, or with MSC's drinks and Wi-Fi package built in — so each household can decide whether the package pays for itself. On this sailing MSC is pricing balcony staterooms at the ocean-view rate.

Sailing B

Symphony of the Seas — Cozumel & the Bahamas, 8 nights

June 19–27, 2027 · Galveston round trip

Symphony is one of the largest ships afloat, and it is organized around neighborhoods rather than decks: an open-air Central Park with real trees down the middle of the ship, a Boardwalk at the stern with a carousel, pools and a sports deck up top. For a family with kids of different ages that matters — you can scatter after breakfast and still find each other.

The route is longer than Sailing A and goes somewhere different. After Cozumel, the ship crosses the Gulf and the Florida Straits to the Bahamas. Perfect Day at CocoCay is Royal Caribbean's private island — beaches, pools and a waterpark; your advisor will confirm which parts of the island are covered by your fare and which, like the waterpark and cabanas, are sold separately. Nassau gives you a city stop: Junkanoo Beach a short walk from the pier, the Queen's Staircase, conch stands at Arawak Cay. Expect more sea days than on Seascape; the exact day-by-day order is to be confirmed by your advisor.

Staterooms on Symphony

Royal Caribbean sells two kinds of rate. A Guarantee promises you a stateroom in that category somewhere on the ship, with the exact room assigned later — sometimes not until shortly before sailing, and the view may be obstructed. A named category lets you choose the room and deck when you book. Several rates below are special rates with a limited number of rooms, so the family's timing matters more here than on Sailing A. All Symphony staterooms have two twin beds that convert to a king, one bathroom, and a sofa bed in rooms that sleep 3 or 4. Accessible staterooms are larger; ask your advisor if anyone in the family needs one.

Deciding together

The honest trade between the two

Seascape is the shorter, lower-priced week, and its fare already carries port fees and taxes. The ports are close to Galveston and close to each other, so the week has an easy shape: two sea days out, three ports in a row, one sea day home. If several households want a balcony, the ocean-view-rate promotion is the thing to look at — a Fantastica balcony for two is $2,015.58, about $120 above the Bella ocean view.

Symphony costs more for a comparable cabin — roughly $1,000 more for the lowest interior for two, before confirming what the fare includes — and asks for an extra night and more sea time in exchange for a bigger ship and a different set of ports. Perfect Day at CocoCay is the kind of stop that works for a mixed-age group, and Nassau is a real city. Most rates come with $50 to spend on board per stateroom.

Neither is the wrong answer. What we'd ask each household to decide is: how many nights, how much ship, and whether drinks and Wi-Fi should be inside the price or bought as you go.
