A plain word about accessibility in Rome

Europe does not handle accessibility the way the United States does, and Rome's historic center is one of the harder places. There is no equivalent here of the ADA standard that American travelers are used to. Italy has accessibility laws, but buildings that are centuries old are often exempt or only partly adapted, and far fewer accommodations are built in or expected.

  • Entrances and elevators. A step or two at the door is common. Elevators are often small, sometimes fitting two people, and can start a few steps above the lobby.
  • Bathrooms. Bathtubs with high sides are still widespread, grab bars are rare, and a walk in shower is never certain until the room is assigned.
  • The word accessible on a listing. It is reported by the hotel itself and means less than it does at home. It describes a feature that exists somewhere in the building, not in every room.
  • Streets. Expect cobblestones, uneven sidewalks, curbs without ramps and slopes. In the restricted traffic zones a taxi may stop a short walk from the door.
  • Restaurants and sights. Restrooms are often down a flight of stairs, and many churches and ruins have steps with no handrail.
  • The people. Hotel staff are usually kind and will carry a bag or offer an arm, but they cannot change the building, and help is given when asked for more often than it is offered.

None of this should spoil the trip. It means choosing a hotel for its street as much as its rooms, using taxis freely, wearing shoes with a good grip, and allowing more time than the map suggests.

Sina Bernini Bristol

The grand hotel on the squarePiazza Barberini 23 · 5 star · Autograph Collection by Marriott · about 166 rooms · founded 1874, fully renovated 2022Visit the hotel website for photos and rooms ↗

The Bristol has stood on Piazza Barberini since 1874, facing Bernini's Triton Fountain, and the square's taxis and morning commuters are part of its daily rhythm. A 2022 renovation refreshed the rooms and the public spaces, and the restaurant on the eighth floor looks out over the rooftops of the old city. It behaves like a proper city hotel: doormen at the entrance, a concierge who knows the neighborhood, and a bistro off the lobby for a quiet plate of pasta on the night nobody wants to go far.

Outside the door

The hotel stands directly on the piazza, which is level. The metro entrance and a taxi rank are a few steps from the door. Via del Tritone runs gently downhill from here to the Trevi Fountain, so the stroll home is a mild climb.

Inside

There is more than one elevator, and Marriott lists the main entrance as accessible. Bathrooms vary between bathtubs and showers, with many rooms now shower only, so a walk in shower can be requested but is not guaranteed.

A tiny townhouse hotel in the grid of shopping streets below the Spanish Steps, a block from Via Condotti. Rooms have wood beamed ceilings, canopy beds and large marble bathrooms. There is no real lobby, and breakfast is brought to the room.

Outside the door

The streets here are flat, and the foot of the Spanish Steps is 3 minutes away. The lanes are narrow and busy with shoppers by day and bars by night. Piazza Barberini is 12 to 15 minutes away.

Inside

The elevator is very small and the staircase is steep and narrow. Several room types have bathtubs. This is a hotel for confident walkers.

Eating, drinking, unwinding

Breakfast is delivered to the room, with a buffet at the sister hotel about 5 minutes away. Restaurants and cafés fill the surrounding streets.

What recent guests say

Staff are praised warmly for luggage help and taxis. Several guests mention nightlife noise.

Good to know

  • Rome charges a city tax, paid at the hotel at check out: about EUR 10 per person per night at 5 star hotels and about EUR 7.50 at 4 star hotels.
  • Every hold on this page can be cancelled without charge until its decision date. After that date the room is either paid or released.
  • Rome's old streets are paved in cobblestones that can be uneven and slick after rain. Shoes with a good grip make a real difference in November.
  • Licensed taxis may enter the restricted traffic zones of the historic center, so a taxi is always an option for the uphill stretch home.
