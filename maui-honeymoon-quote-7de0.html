Where you stay

Sheraton Maui Resort & Spa

Kaʻanapali Beach, built into and around Puʻu Kekaʻa — Black Rock

Kaʻanapali is a three-mile stretch of sand on Maui's west shore, and the Sheraton holds its northern end — the lava point the whole beach bends toward. That position does two things for you. The snorkeling off Black Rock is the best on the strip, a few minutes' swim from the sand, with turtles most mornings. And every evening at sunset, a diver climbs the rock with a torch, lights the tiki torches on the way up, and dives into the sea from the top — a ceremony the resort has kept since 1963. You watch it from the lawn with a drink.

The Kaʻanapali Beachwalk runs south from your door past the other resorts to Whalers Village, about ten minutes on foot, for shops and oceanfront dinners. The pool is a lagoon-style sprawl with a swim-up bar. Rooms are in low-rise wings, so ocean views are real ocean views — not a sliver from a high floor.

Three room categories are available for your dates. All three include the $100 nightly dining credit. The difference is purely how much water you see from bed.

How the week flows

Day by day

A working plan, not a schedule. Every day here can move.

Monday, Sept 21Arrival

Land, drive west, watch the dive

You're on the ground at 12:50 p.m. Collect the Jeep at Kahului — mobile check-in means you're driving inside twenty minutes — and head west on the Honoapiʻilani Highway. It's about 45 minutes to Kaʻanapali; the road hugs the coast through the pali, and if the surf's up at the lookouts you'll see it. Check-in is 4 p.m.; if the room's ready early, they'll hand you the keys. If not, drop bags and get in the water.

Be on the lawn by the rock about half an hour before sunset (around 6:15 p.m.) for the torch-lighting and cliff dive. First dining credit goes to dinner at the resort tonight — you'll be too tired to drive anywhere and you shouldn't have to.

Grocery stop: there's a Safeway and a Foodland in Lahaina/Kaʻanapali; grab water, coffee, and snacks for the room and for the Hana drive.

Tuesday, Sept 22The beach

Black Rock in the morning, nothing in the afternoon

Your body clock will wake you early; use it. The water off Black Rock is clearest and calmest before 9 a.m. — walk in from the sand on the north side of the point, swim out along the rock, and you'll find turtles and reef fish within a few minutes. Gear is available on the beach.

Then do nothing. Pool, beach, a long lunch. Late afternoon, walk the Beachwalk south to Whalers Village and back for sunset. Dinner on the credit, or out at one of the oceanfront tables in the Village — I'll list my picks in your guide.

Wednesday, Sept 23Road to Hana

The long day — with Shaka Guide doing the talking

Leave the resort by 6:30 a.m. with the tank full. It's about an hour to Paʻia (stop for coffee and malasadas), then two and a half to three hours to Hana with the stops the audio guide calls out: bamboo at the arboretum, the black-sand beach at Waiʻānapanapa, the waterfalls that are running that week. Lunch in Hana is a food truck and a view. If you've got the legs, the hike at the end of the road to the bamboo forest is the best thing on the route.

Come back the way you came — rental agreements don't allow the back road, and it's slower anyway. You'll be at the resort by dark, and the dining credit means dinner is handled.

Waiʻānapanapa State Park requires a timed entry reservation for visitors. I'll book it as soon as your dates are confirmed — reservations open 30 days ahead, so we're inside the window now.

Thursday, Sept 24Haleakalā

Sleep in, then go up the volcano for sunset

A slow morning at the beach after yesterday. Around 2 p.m., point the Jeep upcountry: through Kula, then the switchbacks to the summit at 10,023 feet. It's about two hours from Kaʻanapali. Sunset from the top — above the clouds, with the crater going red behind you — needs no reservation, and you'll have it with a fraction of the sunrise crowd. Bring every layer you packed; it's often in the 40s up there with wind.

Dinner on the way down in Kula or Makawao, or back at the resort on the credit.

If you'd rather do sunrise: the park releases a small batch of $1 vehicle reservations 48 hours ahead, at 7 a.m. Hawaii time. I'll try for one on your behalf. If it doesn't come through, sunset is the plan — or a guided sunrise tour, which has its own allocation.

Friday, Sept 25On the water

A morning sail, then a proper honeymoon dinner

A snorkel sail this morning — several catamarans leave right from Kaʻanapali Beach, so there's no drive. Molokini crater is the classic, but in September the Lanaʻi or west-shore sails are just as good and less crowded. Back by early afternoon for the spa, or a last stretch of beach.

Tonight's the one to book: a luau or an oceanfront dinner in Kaʻanapali. Tell me which way you lean and I'll hold it.

Tours and the dinner aren't in this quote. I'll price them once you've picked a room, so nothing gets booked twice.

Saturday, Sept 26Departure

A full last day, then the night flight home

Checkout is 11 a.m.; I'll request a late checkout, but plan on the resort holding your bags. The Jeep gives you the afternoon: the south shore beaches at Wailea, or upcountry to Makawao for lunch and the shops. Stay in the water as long as you like. You need to be back at Kahului to return the Jeep around 6 p.m. for the 8:35 p.m. flight.

You'll land in Las Vegas at 5:05 a.m., connect, and be home at Hobby by 11:40 a.m. Sunday.

Straight talk

Good to know before you say yes

Lahaina, three years on

Kaʻanapali is open and fully operating, but Front Street in Lahaina, five minutes south, is still rebuilding from the 2023 fire. Some businesses have reopened; much has not. Your guide covers what's open now and how to be a good guest in a town that's still healing.

The overnight return

The red-eye through Las Vegas is the trade-off for a full Saturday on Maui. The 95-minute connection is fine on a normal night. If you'd rather a daytime routing at a different price, say so and I'll price it.

Reservations I'll handle

Waiʻānapanapa timed entry, the Haleakalā sunrise attempt if you want it, the snorkel sail, and Friday's dinner. Tell me your preferences and they go in the app with confirmation numbers.

What the Jeep costs beyond the rental

Resort parking, gas (Hana and Haleakalā will use a tank between them), and the Haleakalā park entry of about $30 per vehicle. None of that is in the quote; all of it is worth it.
