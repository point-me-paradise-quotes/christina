I've been there

The reason the Dominican Republic is on this list at all is that I was on this coast recently on a site inspection, and Miches is the most interesting thing happening in the country right now. Everything here is new — the resorts, the road in, the whole development — and the beaches are better than what you'll find on the crowded side of the island. Punta Cana proper has been built out for decades. This stretch hasn't.

I walked through both of these properties on that trip and I couldn't pick a favourite, which almost never happens. One specific: the food at the Hilton was the best I ate on that trip. I'll be honest that a kitchen can have a good week, and I can only tell you what was in front of me — but it was excellent, and I went in without expecting it to be.

One thing to understand before you choose: swimming in Cabo.

The Baja peninsula has almost no continental shelf. The sand drops away sharply a few feet out, which creates rip currents strong enough to pull out experienced swimmers. Resorts post flags, and some station staff who will stop you from entering the water. This isn't excess caution — people have drowned here.

Only a handful of places in the region are safe for ocean swimming: Médano Beach, Chileno Bay, Santa María, Palmilla, and a few protected stretches along the corridor. Of the four Cabo resorts below, one sits on a swimmable beach. Three do not. Nassau, the Dominican Republic, and Costa Rica have no such problem — Cable Beach, Playa Esmeralda, and Playa Conchal are all calm and swimmable.

None of this rules the Cabo options out. They have serious pools, and plenty of couples never get in the ocean. But choose knowing which is which, rather than finding out on the first morning.
