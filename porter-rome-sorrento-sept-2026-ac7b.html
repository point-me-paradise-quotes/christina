The Trip

Two cities, one week, no wasted days

You land at Fiumicino at 7:55 on a Monday morning with a driver waiting and the whole city still ahead of you. Three days later you take a train south, and by late afternoon you're on a cliff road above the water with Vesuvius across the bay.

Rome is built for walking, and you're staying where that works — on Via Flavia, a quiet street off the Via Veneto, with Villa Borghese's gardens a few minutes north and the Trevi Fountain, the Pantheon, and the Spanish Steps all reachable on foot or one stop on the Metro. Evenings are yours: the city stays open late, the piazzas fill after dark, and dinner at nine is normal.

Sorrento is the opposite kind of place. It sits on a cliff above the sea, and your hotel sits on the best stretch of that cliff — Via Capo, where the road runs west out of town and the whole Bay of Naples opens up beneath you. Days here are for the water: Capri is a short ferry ride, the Amalfi road starts twenty minutes down the coast, and Pompeii is closer than most people realize.

Sorrento · 24–27 September

Hotel Minerva

Via Capo 30, 80067 Sorrento

Photographs show the hotel. Sea view and balcony are requested, not guaranteed.

Built in 1878 and still holding its original bones — stone, tile, high ceilings, and a position on the Via Capo cliff that newer hotels can't buy. The rooftop pool is set in blue mosaic with the Bay of Naples directly below and Vesuvius across the water. The restaurant serves on a stone terrace at the same elevation. Breakfast is on that terrace, which is a good enough reason to get up.

Town is a ten to fifteen minute walk downhill along Via Capo, past the old villas and the lemon groves, into Piazza Tasso. The walk back is uphill — worth knowing, and worth a taxi some evenings.

▸Ideas for your two Rome days Colosseum, Vatican, Borghese, food

Colosseum, Roman Forum & Palatine Hill

One ticket covers all three, and they connect. The Forum is where the city actually happened — the Colosseum is the photograph, but the Forum is the story. Arena floor access is worth the upgrade if it's available; standing where the sand was changes the scale of it.

Best early morning · Books out weeks ahead

Vatican Museums & the Sistine Chapel

Miles of galleries ending in one room. Early-entry tours get you into the Sistine Chapel before general admission, which is the difference between seeing the ceiling and standing in a crowd. St. Peter's is next door and free, but the line is long after ten.

Before opening · Books out weeks ahead

Borghese Gallery

Bernini's sculptures in the rooms they were carved for, inside a villa in the middle of Rome's largest park. Entry is by timed two-hour slot and capped, so it never feels crowded. A twenty-minute walk from your hotel through the gardens.

Timed entry · Reserve early

An evening food walk in Trastevere or Testaccio

Trastevere for the atmosphere — cobbles, ivy, restaurants that spill into the street. Testaccio for the food, which is where Romans actually eat. Either one is a good way to spend a night you don't want to plan.

Evening · Small groups

Ready to book — Rome by golf cart

▸Ideas for your two Sorrento days Capri, Amalfi, Pompeii

Capri

Twenty-five minutes by ferry from Marina Piccola. The island empties out after four when the day boats leave, which is the best hour to be there. A private boat around the island reaches the sea caves and swimming spots the buses never get near.

Friday · Go on an early ferry

The Amalfi Drive, with a driver

Positano, Amalfi, and Ravello along a road cut into the cliff face. Do not drive it yourself — the road is narrow, the buses are wide, and parking doesn't exist. With a driver you can stop where you want and look out the window the rest of the time.

Saturday · Full day, private

Pompeii

Larger than most people expect and confusing without a guide — a good one turns a field of walls into streets, shops, and houses with names attached. Roughly half an hour from Sorrento. Go in the morning; there's no shade by noon.

Morning · Guide strongly recommended

A day that doesn't leave Sorrento

A cooking class in a lemon grove above the town, the woodwork studios in the old streets, lunch down at Marina Grande where the fishing boats still land, and the terrace at the hotel for the rest of it. After Rome and Capri, this is the day people say they'd keep.

Either day · Flexible
