The shape of the trip

One night in Papeete to land, then eight on Moorea with nothing to pack up in between.

Brandi, here is the honeymoon as it stands: United from Houston to Tahiti, a first night in Papeete steps from the ferry, and eight nights over the lagoon on Moorea. Everything below is priced for one room and two adults.

Before you choose

Eight nights or six. This quote is built on eight nights on Moorea, and that is what I highly recommend. Six nights is also an option. Flight schedules from Houston line up best with a six- or eight-night Moorea stay, so those are the two lengths worth pricing.

Flying via Los Angeles instead. It can be done, with trade-offs: longer layovers and an early-morning arrival in Tahiti. Hotel check-in is not until the afternoon, and the ferry adds more travel to an already long journey, so it makes for a harder first day. The upside is that a morning arrival lets you skip the Papeete overnight and go straight to Moorea if you'd prefer.

A rental car on Moorea. Highly recommended. I add this separately, and it is paid on the island, so it is not part of the package rates below.

Boutique Hotel Kon Tiki Tahiti

Central Papeete, a short walk from the Moorea ferry.

After fourteen hours of travel, the job of this night is simple: a short ride from the airport, a king bed, and the ferry terminal a few minutes away on foot the next morning. Kon Tiki sits on Boulevard de la Reine Pomare IV in the middle of Papeete, about ten minutes by car from the airport.

There are two places to eat upstairs — SURFHOUSE, and the REEFTOP rooftop bar, both looking out over the water. Each is open select days and takes reservations, so I'll check what is serving on a Saturday night before you go.

A minibus van meets you at the airport on arrival evening and takes you to the hotel; that transfer is in every package. The ferry terminal is walkable the next morning. If you'd rather not walk with luggage, I can add a short transfer to the ferry.

How the three resorts compare

The same nine questions asked of each, with the trade-offs left in.

Where it sits

HiltonNorth coast, on a point between Cook's Bay and Opunohu Bay. A central base for the island's interior, with the Belvedere lookout and both bays close by. It catches both sunrise and sunset.
ManavaNorth coast beside the village of Maharepa, about two miles from Cook's Bay. Shops, casual restaurants, and a bank are within walking distance. You get both sunrises and sunsets from the overwater bungalows.
SofitelNortheast coast at Temae, looking across the channel to Tahiti. This is the sunrise side of the island. It is the farthest of the three from the bays and the island's central activity; Sofitel puts the Belvedere about 15 minutes away by car.

Arrival and departure days

HiltonThe longest transfer of the three, roughly 20 minutes by car from the arrival side of the island.
ManavaA short drive from the ferry dock, between the other two.
SofitelAbout five minutes from the ferry dock, the shortest transfer of the three.

Your bungalow, as quoted

HiltonKing Panoramic Overwater Bungalow, 667 sq ft, the largest of the three. One of 54 overwater bungalows; the Panoramic category sits farther out on the pontoon for wider views and more privacy. Glass floor panel, and steps from the deck into the lagoon.
ManavaPremium Overwater Bungalow, 517 sq ft. The Premium category faces the open lagoon, where the standard overwater bungalows face the beach and mountains. King bed plus a twin, a glass-bottom viewing table, and direct lagoon access. Finishes are simpler than at the two larger resorts.
SofitelSuperior Villa, Overwater, 334 sq ft, the smallest of the three. Interiors were renovated in 2026. Direct lagoon access from the deck. Neighboring villas sit close enough to be part of the view.

The beach

HiltonA white-sand beach with loungers and umbrellas. It is on the small side.
ManavaA small, narrow private beach, with the overwater bungalows lining the water in front of it.
SofitelThe strongest beach of the three: Temae, the island's longest stretch of white sand. It adjoins the public beach, so it feels less enclosed than the other two.

Snorkeling from your deck

HiltonGood, not the best. There are plenty of fish around the bungalows, but the coral beneath them has seen bleaching. I'd put it below the Sofitel.
ManavaIt varies. The resort keeps a coral nursery, and reef sharks and turtles do pass by the bungalows, but this is the least consistent of the three. For rays and sharks up close, I'd add a lagoon excursion.
SofitelThe best of the three for snorkeling straight from the bungalow, with coral in good condition.

Dining

HiltonThree restaurants and two bars, including the creperie set over the water. The most variety on site for an eight-night stay.
ManavaTwo restaurants and two bars on site, plus the village restaurants within walking distance.
SofitelTwo restaurants, PURE and K. Sofitel's own site currently lists K as unavailable; I'll confirm its status for March. Dinner options on site are limited, and other restaurants need a car or taxi.

Feel and upkeep

HiltonA full-service resort of about 104 bungalows, with families and groups as well as couples. The bungalows are comfortable, though they are showing some age.
ManavaA four-star resort of about 90 rooms, Polynesian in style. It is the best value of the three, and it stays quiet despite the central location.
SofitelAbout 110 villas. Common areas and overwater villas were renovated in 2026. Garden villas are being upgraded in sections through August 2027, so some work may be under way during your stay; I'll confirm what is scheduled for March.

The strongest case for it

HiltonThe largest bungalow, the most dining on site, and a central base for seeing the island.
ManavaThe lowest price, a village you can walk to, and a bungalow facing the open lagoon.
SofitelCrystal-blue water and some of the best snorkeling on the lagoon, plus the beach, the newest rooms, and the easiest arrival and departure.

The main trade-off

HiltonThe longest transfer, a smaller beach, and snorkeling a step below the Sofitel's.
ManavaSimpler finishes, the smallest beach, and a breakfast that ends at 8:30 AM.
SofitelThe highest price for the smallest room, and fewer dinner options with the rest of the island a drive away. It also loses the light earlier: the afternoon sun drops behind the mountain that sits right behind the resort.

This is my own read of the three, with the trade-offs left in. Nothing here is ranked; the right resort depends on what matters most to the two of you.

Hilton Moorea Lagoon Resort and Spa

Over the lagoon on the north coast, between the island's two bays.

Est. $14,654 · Est. $7,327 per personSelected

The Hilton sits on Moorea's north coast between Cook's Bay and Opunohu Bay — 104 bungalows spread through the gardens, along the beach, and out over the water on pontoons. It is a full resort, not a hideaway: three restaurants, two bars, a pool, a spa, tennis courts, and a beach with loungers and umbrellas already set out.

Dinner has range for an eight-night stay. Arii Vahine is the French dining room and where breakfast is served; Rotui Grill & Bar handles lunch and dinner on the beach with your feet near the sand; and Toatea Creperie & Bar is the open-air spot over the water. Kayaks and snorkel gear go out straight from the property.

The honest trade: this is a family-friendly resort, with a children's menu and kids' activities on offer, not an adults-only property. The overwater bungalows put you out over the lagoon and away from the pool, which is where most of that energy stays.

Option 2 · Overwater bungalow · 1 room · 2 adults · 8 nights

Manava Beach Resort & Spa Moorea

Polynesian in style, beside the village of Maharepa.

Est. $12,255 · Est. $6,128 per personSelected

Manava sits on the north shore next to Maharepa, about two miles from Cook's Bay. It is the smaller of the two resorts, around 90 rooms and bungalows, and the village is the point: boutiques, casual restaurants, and a bank are within walking distance, so dinner does not have to happen on property every night.

On site there are two restaurants. Mahanai serves breakfast and dinner, and Autera'a handles lunch by the pool. The Matiehani Rum Bar is on the beach and opens select days. In the lagoon out front the resort keeps a coral nursery, To'a Nui, and there is scuba diving, kayaking, and snorkeling from the property.

The honest trade: the bungalow is 150 sq ft smaller than the Hilton's, there are two restaurants where the Hilton has three, and breakfast ends at 8:30 AM. In return you are walking distance from a real village, and the package comes in Est. $2,399 lower.

Option 3 · Overwater villa · 1 room · 2 adults · 8 nights

Sofitel Kia Ora Moorea Beach Resort

Temae's white sand on the northeast coast, facing Tahiti across the channel.

Est. $14,766 · Est. $7,383 per personSelected

The Sofitel is on the northeast coast at Temae, on a long white-sand beach with Tahiti's outline across the water. It is the closest of the three to where you arrive, about five minutes from the Vaiare ferry dock, and it reopened in 2026 after a renovation of its common areas and overwater villas.

There are two restaurants. PURE is on the beach and serves breakfast, lunch, and dinner. K is the dinner-only dining room looking out over the ocean; Sofitel's own site currently lists it as unavailable, so I'll confirm its status for March. Vue, the cocktail bar above the pool, serves lunch, dinner, and lighter plates, and there is a second bar down on the sand. The spa has five treatment rooms and outdoor treatment areas, and yoga classes run on site.

The honest trade: this is the smallest bungalow of the three at 334 sq ft and the highest package price. Cook's Bay and Opunohu Bay are a drive away, not out your door. What you get for it is the beach, the newest overwater rooms, and the shortest transfer on arrival and departure days.
