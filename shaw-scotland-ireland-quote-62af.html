The shape of the trip

Two countries, one direction of travel — north into the Highlands, then west across the water.

You land in Edinburgh and spend two nights on foot, because nothing you want to see there needs a car. The car arrives on the third morning, and from there it's the A9 north through Perthshire, then west toward Skye, then south through Glencoe.

From Glasgow you fly to Dublin, pick up a second car, and drive north to Belfast for the Causeway Coast and the Titanic yards. Then the long run south to Kenmare, where the driving stops for three nights and the days finally open up. A last night near Shannon puts you twenty-five minutes from the flight home.

We don't book hotels. We build trips. Custom guides, trip app, real human when something goes sideways. The work you'd hate doing — already done.
The flow

What the trip feels like

One continuous line, north then west. You never double back on yourself.

The first week keeps moving — one or two nights in each place, a new view out the window every morning. That's deliberate; Scotland rewards covering ground. Then the pace breaks. You land in Kerry with three nights in one room, nothing booked, and the car parked. By the time you get there you'll want exactly that.

Day by day

How it actually goes

Fourteen days, arranged so the long drives land on transfer days and the good days stay open.
Day 1Fri 25 Sep

Land in Edinburgh

You come in off the overnight and the day is deliberately empty. The hotel sits four minutes from Princes Street on the airport tram line, so there's no car, no parking, no queue — you drop the bags and walk. Eat somewhere close and go to bed early.

Day 2Sat 26 Sep

Edinburgh, on foot

The morning is yours — Dean Village is twenty minutes away on the Water of Leith and feels nothing like a capital city, or Arthur's Seat if the legs have recovered. At one o'clock a storyteller takes you through the wynds and closes of the Old Town and walks you straight over the drawbridge into the Castle. You'll be inside until late afternoon.

Day 3Sun 27 Sep

North on the A9 to Inverness

Collect the car and head for the Highlands. Blair Castle sits directly on the route at Pitlochry — the Duke of Atholl still keeps a private army there — and the distillery is eight miles further on. You reach Inverness late afternoon. That evening, two of the better-known names in Scottish traditional music pour you Glenmorangie, Dalmore, Tomatin, Glen Ord and Dalwhinnie and tell you where each one comes from.

Day 4Mon 28 Sep

The Isle of Skye

West through Glen Shiel to Eilean Donan, sitting on its own island where three lochs meet, then over the bridge onto Skye. The Old Man of Storr, the Quiraing, Kilt Rock, lunch on the harbour front at Portree. This is the biggest day of the Scottish half, and how you spend it is the one real choice in the trip — see the options below.

Day 5Tue 29 Sep

Down Loch Ness to Fort William

An easy morning. You drive the length of Loch Ness and stop at Fort Augustus for a fifty-minute cruise — live sonar sweeping the water beneath you, the abbey rising off the shoreline. You're in Fort William by early afternoon with the day still open: Glenfinnan and its viaduct are seventeen miles west, and Ben Nevis is ten minutes from the door.

Day 6Wed 30 Sep

Glencoe, Loch Lomond and Stirling

The best driving day in Scotland. Glencoe first, where the road runs between walls of rock, then the long western shore of Loch Lomond. Stirling Castle is worth ninety minutes — seven battlefields are visible from the ramparts — and the Kelpies stand a hundred feet high at Falkirk on the way past. You finish at Glasgow Airport, because tomorrow starts early.

Day 7Thu 1 Oct

Fly to Dublin, drive to Belfast

Glasgow to Dublin at 8:35, wheels down at 9:50. You collect the Irish car and drive north — two hours, and you're in Belfast by early afternoon. That leaves the Titanic yards for the same day: nine galleries on the exact slipway where she was built, plus the SS Nomadic, her original tender ship and the last White Star vessel afloat.

Day 8Fri 2 Oct

The Causeway Coast

An hour north and the coast road opens up. Forty thousand basalt columns stepping down into the Atlantic, with a guided tour and audio guide included. Then Carrick-a-Rede if the nerve holds, Dunluce Castle falling off its headland, and the Dark Hedges on the way back — a beech avenue planted in the 1700s that you'll recognise the moment you see it.

Day 9Sat 3 Oct

South to Cork

The long one — most of the length of the island. We've kept the day otherwise clear so you can break it where you like; the Rock of Cashel sits roughly halfway if you want a reason to stop. You arrive in Cork in the evening, ten minutes' walk from a city that takes its food seriously.

Day 10Sun 4 Oct

Fota, then west to Kenmare

Fota Wildlife Park is twenty minutes east of Cork on its own island — seventy acres where most of the animals aren't behind anything. Give it the morning, then drive west into Kerry. You arrive at the estate in the afternoon and the car can stay where it is for two days.

Day 11Mon 5 Oct

Kenmare and Killarney

The spa in the morning. Then out along the N71 through Moll's Gap to Ladies View, named for Queen Victoria's ladies-in-waiting who stopped there in 1861 and wouldn't move on. Killarney National Park runs to twenty-six thousand acres below it, with Muckross House at the centre.

Day 12Tue 6 Oct

The Ring of Kerry

A hundred and ten miles around the Iveragh Peninsula, and Kenmare is the right place to start it. Drive it clockwise — the tour coaches all run counterclockwise, and you'd rather see them coming than meet them head-on for four hours. Sneem, Waterville and Glenbeigh are the villages worth stopping in.

Day 13Wed 7 Oct

North to Ennis

Two and a half hours up through Adare, thatched roofs the length of the main street. Ennis has something close to a hundred pubs and a serious reputation for traditional music — and the best-known session bar in town is inside your hotel, so the last night in Ireland needs no planning at all.

Day 14Thu 8 Oct

Shannon, and home

Twenty-five minutes to the airport. Drop the car and fly.
