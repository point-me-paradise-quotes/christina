The Trip

West to east, Spain to Italy, the long way round

You board in Barcelona and step off ten nights later at Civitavecchia, having crossed most of the Mediterranean to get there. It's the reverse of what people expect — rather than starting in Italy and working east, this one runs the length of the sea and delivers you back to Rome at the end.

The middle of it is the Greek Isles, properly done. Athens for twelve hours, which is enough for the Acropolis and its museum without rushing. Santorini until 10:00 p.m. — the caldera at sunset, which is the entire reason to go and something day-callers never see. Ephesus, Mykonos, and Olympia, where the ancient games were held. Valletta comes early, and the ship berths in the Grand Harbour directly beneath the bastion walls.

Three sea days are built in, spaced to break up the long legs rather than clustered at one end. On a ship this size that's a genuine part of the trip, not filler.

We don't book hotels. We build trips. Custom guides, trip app, real human when something goes sideways. The work you'd hate doing — already done.

Two tender ports. At Santorini and Mykonos the ship anchors offshore and you come in by small boat. It's straightforward in normal conditions, but worth knowing in advance if anyone in the group would rather not manage a tender — and tendering can be cancelled in rough weather, which is a decision the captain makes on the day.

Port times are as scheduled and may vary by departure date, weather, safety or operational considerations.

The second of Celebrity's five Solstice-class ships, launched in 2009 and taken through the line's full Celebrity Revolution refit in 2019. She carries 2,852 guests across 14 decks — mid-sized by current standards, which is the useful size: enough to carry a full theatre, a spa and a genuine range of restaurants, small enough that you aren't queuing forty minutes to get off in port.

The top deck

Half an acre of real, growing grass — the Lawn Club, and the thing Solstice-class ships have always been known for. Lawn games, open-air concerts and the best place on the ship to watch a coastline go by. One note: Celebrity is rolling out a redesigned version of this deck, called Sunset Park, across the class. Equinox's turn hasn't been dated yet, so I'll confirm which version you'll sail with as we get closer.

Eating well

Tuscan Grille and Murano are the two specialty rooms worth booking, both for a surcharge. Craft Social handles beer and comfort food. The Martini Bar's flair bartenders are a performance in their own right, and the ice-topped bar is where the ship gathers before dinner.

The Spa

A full treatment menu plus the Persian Garden, a thermal suite of aromatic steam rooms and heated stone loungers. Both are available to book — worth reserving on a port day, when the place is close to empty.

Connected

Starlink high-speed internet across the ship. Also on board: the Sunset Bar at the stern, a casino, the theatre, The Library, and a Silent Disco that is far better than it sounds.

This is the only category on the page that changes what your days look like rather than just where you sleep. AquaClass is Celebrity's spa tier, and it comes with two things no veranda stateroom gets:

  • Blu — a restaurant reserved for AquaClass guests only. Lighter cooking, no queue, no assigned seating time, and small enough that the staff know you by the second night. On a ten-night sailing that's a different rhythm to your evenings, not just a nicer table.
  • The Persian Garden — included access to the spa's thermal suite, a set of aromatic steam rooms and heated stone loungers. Everyone else pays for it.
  • Spa-inspired stateroom amenities and a private veranda, same as the categories above

Thirty staterooms are open on Deck 11, and plenty of them are midship — so three rooms together is comfortably achievable here. Deck 11 is quiet: an accommodation deck rather than a thoroughfare, with The Library aft of the staterooms and the Relaxation Lounge forward.
